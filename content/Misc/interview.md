Intro: the structure of a KAN is superficially similar to an MLP, with multiple layers of neurons, each with weights, and nonlinearities.
- let's first look at the structure of an MLP. It consists of, again, multiple layers of neurons. each neuron has a weight/bias and a nonlinearity associated with it (maybe explain why nonlinearities are needed). The weights are the parameters we train in the model, while the activation function is fixed. (give examples) The basic principle that MLPs operate on is the Universal Approximation Theorem, which states that any real-valued continuous function can be approximated by a feedforward neural network with a single hidden layer and an activation function.
- A KAN, as opposed to this, consists again of multiple layers, usually 3, we'll get into why later, with fixed weights, and trainable activation functions.
K-A Theorem: The Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem states that any multivariate function can be represented as a finite composition of univariate functions and additions.
