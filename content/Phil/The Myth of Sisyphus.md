Why shouldn’t you kill yourself?  
Camus says the question of why life is worth living is the fundamental question of philosophy, and all other questions, be it philosophical or scientific, come after.  
Objectively from a non-anthropocentric point of view I disagree, because the universe, and with it the fundamental question of existence, came before humans did  
From our point of view though, it does make sense. IMO the fundamental question of existence is why it exists, and thus naturally the fundamental question of human existence should be why we exist.  

“Rarely is suicide committed through reflection” so true  
Of course, the environment which leads to a person questioning their existence also provides them with ample external means to crystallize their inner \[angst]

sidenote: Human suicide is more meaningful than animal suicide, because humans have a means of justifying their own existence, and (i’ll expand on this later, if it has any legs)

In my view there are three ways you can react to this notion of absolute insignificance;  
disproving it (will talk about this in another note), compromising the integrity of the worldview, and accepting it for what it is.  
the third reaction inevitably results in suicide, because absolute nihilism and a desire to exist are simply incompatible notions  
the second reaction is what basically all humans have, i.e. using delusion as a tool to escape having to tackle the notion. Delusion doesn’t necessarily need to come in the form of religion, and i would go as far as to say that any reason to live we give ourselves is part of this delusion, because again, facing the truth absolutely logically results in suicide.  
The first is something i will attempt to do, because logically we have not found a limit to the potential of intelligent life, and maybe we can reach a point where we are no longer absolutely insignificant in relation to the universe.  
addition: maybe just proving that this can be possible theoretically in a like self-consistent way is enough, instead of seeing it all the way through actually doing it?

Of course, this says nothing about the smaller insignificance of the individual’s miniscule role in society, and this notion will probably continue to exist as long as society is comprised of \[personal] individuals as opposed to any variant of a non-individualistic society (hive-mind)
