## Why:
**Value Based**: You are in a state, you approximate what reward you would get for each action you take from that state, then epsilon-greedily choose the action with the most expected reward
**Policy Based**: You are in a state, you directly stochastically pick from a probability distribution of actions.
## $\theta_{k+1} = \theta_k + \alpha \nabla_{\theta} J(\pi_{\theta})|_{\theta_k}$ 
*where* J = $E_{\tau \approx \pi_{\theta}}[R(\tau)]$.
## Advantages:
- Works with really large or continuous action spaces, where estimating the expected reward for each action is absurd or impossible.
- Because of the probability distribution nature, you can learn stochastic policies
## Naive Gradient Ascent (Finite Differences):
Simplest idea: perturb the parameters a little in each dimension of the action space, calculate gradient in each direction

**Disadvantages:** really inefficient computationally as the action space increases in dimensionality
## Log-derivative Trick:
# $\nabla_{\theta} \pi_{\theta} (s, a) = \pi_{\theta} (s, a) \frac{\nabla_{\theta} \pi_{\theta} (s, a)}{\pi_{\theta} (s, a)}$ 
# $= \pi_{\theta} (s, a) \nabla_{\theta} log \pi_{\theta} (s, a)$ 

This greatly simplifies differentiation, as taking the gradient of $P(\tau | \theta)$ (which is a product) gives you a sum,ct or quotient rule
